Vinyl plank flooring can be estimated by individual plank dimensions or by the square-foot coverage printed on each box. A plank count helps explain the material quantity; exact box coverage is usually the more direct purchasing input.

## Measure the complete floor area

Calculate length × width for every rectangular section, then add the areas that will receive the same product. Include applicable closets and alcoves. Split L-shaped spaces instead of measuring only one side.

For an 11-foot by 13-foot room:

**11 ft × 13 ft = 143 sq ft**

See [how to measure a room](/guides/how-to-measure-a-room-for-flooring) or the [multiple-room guide](/guides/how-to-calculate-flooring-for-multiple-rooms) when the floor has several sections.

## Calculate by plank dimensions

Convert plank dimensions to feet, then multiply length by width.

A plank measuring 36 inches by 7.25 inches is 3 feet by 7.25 ÷ 12 foot:

**3 ft × (7.25 ÷ 12) ft = 1.8125 sq ft per plank**

The 143-square-foot room requires before allowance:

**143 ÷ 1.8125 = 78.897 planks**

If the selected project allowance is 8% in this example:

**143 ÷ 1.8125 × 1.08 = 85.208 planks**

The whole-plank estimate rounds up to 86 planks.

The percentage is an editable example, not a universal vinyl-plank requirement. Confirm the amount for the room, layout, product, and installation instructions.

### Example with a fractional plank count

A 165-square-foot floor using planks that cover 2.2 square feet each gives:

**165 ÷ 2.2 = 75 planks before allowance**

With an entered 7% allowance:

**75 × 1.07 = 80.25 planks**

The whole-plank estimate rounds up to 81 planks.

## Calculate boxes from package coverage

For purchasing, begin with required area after allowance. If the 143-square-foot room uses the example 8% value:

**143 × 1.08 = 154.44 sq ft required**

If the exact box covers 22.5 square feet:

**154.44 ÷ 22.5 = 6.864 boxes**

Round up to 7 whole boxes. They provide 157.5 square feet of stated coverage. That package total may not equal the plank-count estimate if the package uses product-specific or nominal coverage conventions; use the manufacturer’s current figure for the purchase calculation.

## Why box coverage is useful

Actual product boxes can differ in plank count and coverage even when products appear similar. Enter the figure from the exact product rather than assuming a standard vinyl-plank box. Review [how box coverage works](/guides/flooring-box-carton-coverage) before converting the project total.

## Layout considerations

Area and piece formulas cannot predict every usable cut. Actual needs can change with:

- Plank direction and the starting wall
- Minimum end-piece lengths or stagger rules in the instructions
- Narrow final rows
- Doorways, closets, transitions, and floor registers
- Connected rooms with different orientations
- Pattern or visual-repeat requirements
- Damaged locking edges or unacceptable pieces

These conditions inform the allowance or require a layout check; they do not change the underlying area formula.

Underlayment, moisture barriers, adhesive, trim, reducers, and transition pieces are separate materials unless the specific product system states otherwise.

## Common mistakes

- Omitting closets or small connected floor sections
- Mixing inches and feet when calculating plank area
- Using dimensions or coverage from a different product
- Rounding individual room totals before combining them
- Applying allowance twice
- Rounding a fractional box down
- Treating every offcut as reusable
- Ignoring manufacturer layout and subfloor requirements

A tile estimate has four distinct steps: calculate the surface area, calculate one tile’s area, account for the project’s chosen allowance, and convert the result to whole tiles or boxes. Keep the units consistent and delay whole-number rounding until the purchase quantity is calculated.

## 1. Measure the tiled surface

For a rectangular floor or wall:

**Surface area = length × width**

A 10-foot by 12-foot floor is 120 square feet. Divide L-shaped floors, shower walls, or backsplashes into separate rectangles and add the non-overlapping areas. The [room measuring guide](/guides/how-to-measure-a-room-for-flooring) explains irregular spaces and feet-and-inches conversion.

## 2. Calculate one tile’s area

Convert tile dimensions to the same unit used for the surface. For dimensions in inches:

**Tile area in square feet = tile length in inches × tile width in inches ÷ 144**

There are 144 square inches in one square foot.

| Tile dimensions | Tile area |
| --- | ---: |
| 12 in × 12 in | 1 sq ft |
| 6 in × 24 in | 1 sq ft |
| 12 in × 24 in | 2 sq ft |
| 18 in × 18 in | 2.25 sq ft |

Use actual product dimensions when available. Nominal tile sizes and manufacturer coverage may not match a simple dimension calculation exactly.

## 3. Calculate tiles before allowance

**Tiles before allowance = surface area ÷ tile area**

For a 120-square-foot floor using 12-inch by 24-inch tiles:

**120 ÷ 2 = 60 tiles**

For a 105-square-foot surface using 18-inch by 18-inch tiles:

**105 ÷ 2.25 = 46.667 tiles**

Do not round that intermediate result down. The exact value must still be combined with the selected allowance.

## 4. Apply the project allowance and round whole tiles

If the selected allowance for the 120-square-foot example is 10%, the arithmetic is:

**60 × 1.10 = 66 tiles**

Here, 10% is an example only. Tile size, breakage risk, room shape, layout, pattern, and product or installer instructions can require a different value. See the full [flooring allowance guide](/guides/how-much-extra-flooring-to-buy).

For the 46.667-tile example, an entered 8% allowance gives:

**46.667 × 1.08 = 50.4 tiles**

The whole-tile requirement rounds up to 51 tiles.

## Convert tiles or area to boxes

If a box contains eight of the 12-inch by 24-inch tiles and 66 tiles are required:

**66 ÷ 8 = 8.25 boxes**

That requires 9 whole boxes. When the manufacturer provides square-foot coverage per box, use that exact figure for the area-to-box calculation rather than assuming coverage from nominal dimensions. The [box and carton coverage guide](/guides/flooring-box-carton-coverage) explains the difference.

## Openings and areas without tile

It can be mathematically reasonable to subtract a large area that definitely will not be tiled, such as a clearly defined wall opening. Small obstacles and cutouts may still consume tile because surrounding pieces must be cut and offcuts may not be reusable.

Do not subtract cabinets, tubs, vanities, or other fixed elements until the installation boundary is confirmed. For wall tile, measure each wall section and decide explicitly how doors, windows, niches, and backsplashes are treated.

## Layout factors the area formula cannot resolve

The calculation does not determine:

- Centered cuts and balanced border sizes
- Diagonal or repeating patterns
- Vein or image matching
- Grout-joint layout
- Breakage during transport or cutting
- Whether offcuts fit another location
- Trim, edge, bullnose, or specialty pieces

It also does not calculate grout, thinset, adhesive, spacers, membranes, or substrate preparation. Use the relevant product instructions for those materials.

## Common mistakes

- Using inches as though they were feet
- Dividing by 12 instead of 144 when converting square inches to square feet
- Rounding the tile count before allowance is applied
- Rounding a final tile or box quantity down
- Using nominal dimensions when the package states different coverage
- Subtracting every opening or obstacle without considering cuts
- Assuming the area calculation has planned the tile layout

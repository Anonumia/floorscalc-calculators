Calculating several rooms is a record-keeping problem as much as an area problem. Give every room, closet, hallway, and irregular section its own line, then combine only the spaces that will use the same flooring product.

## 1. Define the project boundary

Before measuring, decide which spaces are part of this order. Two adjacent rooms may need separate totals if they use different products, colors, lot numbers, installation patterns, or allowance decisions. Areas using the same product can usually be grouped for an initial package estimate.

Sketch the project and label each space. Include small connecting areas such as halls, landings, door recesses, and closets when they will receive flooring.

## 2. Divide each space into rectangles

Calculate a rectangular area with:

**Area = length × width**

Split an L-shaped or irregular room into non-overlapping rectangles. For detailed measuring methods, including feet and inches, see [how to measure a room for flooring](/guides/how-to-measure-a-room-for-flooring).

## 3. Keep an area schedule

Write down the calculation for every section instead of recording only a final total. This makes it possible to find a missing closet or a duplicated hallway later.

### Worked multi-room project

One product will be installed in two bedrooms, a hall, and two closets.

| Space | Dimensions | Area |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Bedroom 1 | 12 ft × 11 ft | 132 sq ft |
| Bedroom 1 closet | 6 ft × 2 ft | 12 sq ft |
| Bedroom 2 | 10 ft × 10 ft | 100 sq ft |
| Bedroom 2 closet | 5 ft × 2 ft | 10 sq ft |
| Hall | 12 ft × 3 ft | 36 sq ft |
| **Measured total** |  | **290 sq ft** |

The combined measured floor area is:

**132 + 12 + 100 + 10 + 36 = 290 sq ft**

## 4. Apply the chosen allowance

After checking the measured total, apply the project’s selected allowance. If the selected value for this example is 8%:

**290 × 0.08 = 23.2 sq ft allowance**

**290 + 23.2 = 313.2 sq ft required**

The 8% value is an example, not a general recommendation. Room geometry, product, layout, manufacturer requirements, and installer guidance can call for a different value. Use the [flooring allowance guide](/guides/how-much-extra-flooring-to-buy) to separate that decision from the arithmetic.

## 5. Convert required area to whole packages

Use the coverage printed for the exact product. If a carton covers 22.6 square feet:

**313.2 ÷ 22.6 = 13.858 cartons**

Round the final purchase quantity up to 14 cartons. Those cartons cover:

**14 × 22.6 = 316.4 sq ft**

Do not round each room to a carton before combining them unless the rooms truly require separate orders. Doing so can overstate the total because partial coverage from one carton may be available elsewhere in the same project. Whether cut pieces can actually be reused still depends on layout and product rules.

## When to calculate rooms separately

Separate totals may be appropriate when:

- Different flooring products or lot requirements apply
- Rooms use different installation patterns or directions
- Work will occur in separate phases and material will not be shared
- A transition creates distinct layouts
- Product instructions or an installer require room-specific planning
- Carpet roll orientation and seams must be planned for each connected area

Area grouping does not prove that pieces or remnants can be used efficiently across rooms. It provides a material-area total, not a cut plan.

## Avoid overlapping and missing areas

On the sketch, mark each shape after adding it to the schedule. A hallway should not be included both as part of a room’s outer rectangle and as a separate line. Conversely, the gap at a doorway should not disappear because two room measurements stop on opposite sides of it.

For fixed cabinets, islands, hearths, or other exclusions, confirm the intended installation boundary before subtracting anything. A mathematical subtraction is valid only when that floor area will not use the material.

## Mixed feet and inches

Convert inches to decimal feet before multiplying. For example, 9 feet 6 inches becomes 9.5 feet. Keep the precise result for each room and round only the displayed values or final whole purchase quantity.

If some measurements are metric, convert the entire applicable project to one measurement system before adding areas. Square feet and square meters cannot be added directly.

## Common mistakes

- Grouping rooms that will use different products
- Forgetting closets, halls, or door recesses
- Counting the same connecting space twice
- Adding linear dimensions instead of calculated areas
- Applying allowance before the measured project is complete
- Rounding each room or carton calculation independently
- Using package coverage from a similar but different product
- Treating a combined-area estimate as a verified layout plan

Box or carton coverage is the floor area the packaged product is intended to cover. It connects a project’s required area to a purchase quantity. Coverage is product-specific: plank size, tile size, board width, piece count, and packaging can all change it.

## Find the correct coverage

Use the coverage printed on the exact product box, carton, manufacturer page, or current product specification. Check that the product name, size, and unit match what you plan to buy. Do not assume that two similar colors, collections, or plank sizes contain the same coverage.

Package coverage may be stated in square feet or square meters. Calculate the project in the same area unit, or convert before dividing.

## The package formula

After measuring the project and applying the chosen material allowance:

**Packages needed = required area ÷ coverage per package**

Round the final result up to the next whole package. A fraction of a package cannot provide all of the calculated area.

### Worked example

A project has 259.2 square feet of required flooring after its selected allowance. Each carton covers 21.5 square feet.

**259.2 ÷ 21.5 = 12.056 cartons**

The purchase quantity is 13 whole cartons.

| Quantity | Calculation | Area |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Required area | Measured area plus selected allowance | 259.2 sq ft |
| 12 cartons | 12 × 21.5 | 258 sq ft |
| 13 cartons | 13 × 21.5 | 279.5 sq ft |

Twelve cartons fall 1.2 square feet short of the required target, so the fractional result must be rounded up.

## Required area versus purchased coverage

In the example, the required area is 259.2 square feet and the purchased coverage is 279.5 square feet. The difference is:

**279.5 − 259.2 = 20.3 sq ft**

That difference results from whole-carton sizing. It does not mean the allowance was recalculated. Keep these three values distinct:

1. Measured floor area
2. Required area after the chosen allowance
3. Coverage supplied by whole packages

## Why coverage can differ from a piece calculation

You can sometimes estimate package coverage by multiplying the area of one piece by the number of pieces. However, manufacturer-stated coverage is preferable for purchasing because nominal product dimensions, actual dimensions, packaging, and stated coverage conventions may differ.

If a box contains ten planks that are each exactly 4 feet by 6 inches, the calculated area would be:

**4 ft × 0.5 ft × 10 = 20 sq ft**

Before relying on that result, compare it with the coverage stated for the exact box. Use the manufacturer’s current figure when it differs or when dimensions are nominal.

## Round only at the package step

Keep room areas, allowance, and division results at full precision. Rounding each room or intermediate value upward can inflate the order.

For several rooms using the same product, combine exact areas, apply the selected allowance, divide once by package coverage, and then round the package count. Separate orders may be necessary when products, lots, phases, or layouts differ.

## Coverage is not every material needed

Flooring package coverage does not automatically include underlayment, adhesive, grout, fasteners, moisture control, trim, transitions, stair material, or installation accessories. Those products have their own coverage rules and requirements.

Nor does a package count verify board direction, pattern alignment, seam placement, or whether every cut piece can be reused. It is an area-to-package conversion.

## Product and order checks

Before purchasing:

- Confirm coverage on the exact current package
- Confirm square feet versus square meters
- Check whether all packages should come from a consistent production lot
- Review manufacturer installation and storage instructions
- Decide whether unopened packages can be returned
- Ask whether retained material is needed for future repairs
- Recheck the measured area and selected allowance

Lot matching, return policies, and spare-material decisions vary by product and seller; they are not determined by the package formula.

## Common mistakes

- Using coverage from a different size or collection
- Dividing measured area by coverage before applying the selected allowance
- Rounding the required area and package division at several stages
- Rounding a fractional package down
- Treating package overage as an additional allowance percentage
- Mixing square feet and square meters
- Assuming box coverage includes accessories or proves an efficient layout

When a hardwood product is sold with stated area coverage, calculate the purchase quantity from the required area and the coverage listed for its carton, box, or bundle. A board-count estimate is meaningful only when board dimensions are fixed and known; random-length products do not support one precise board count from area alone.

## Measure and combine the floor area

Calculate each rectangular section with length × width, then add the areas receiving the same hardwood product.

A 13-foot by 17-foot room has:

**13 ft × 17 ft = 221 sq ft**

Include applicable closets, bays, and connected sections. Use the [multiple-room guide](/guides/how-to-calculate-flooring-for-multiple-rooms) when the order covers several spaces.

## Apply the project allowance

If this example uses an entered 8% allowance:

**221 × 1.08 = 238.68 sq ft required**

That value demonstrates the formula; it is not a universal hardwood requirement. Board direction, room shape, installation pattern, product grade and specifications, acceptable pieces, and installer guidance can affect the selected allowance. Consult the [flooring allowance guide](/guides/how-much-extra-flooring-to-buy) and the product instructions.

## Convert required area to cartons

If the selected product is sold in cartons covering 22.75 square feet each:

**238.68 ÷ 22.75 = 10.491 cartons**

Round up to 11 whole cartons. Purchased coverage is:

**11 × 22.75 = 250.25 sq ft**

For a second example, 310 square feet of required area with 23.5-square-foot cartons gives:

**310 ÷ 23.5 = 13.191 cartons**, so the purchase quantity is **14 cartons**.

Always use coverage from the exact selected product. Hardwood carton coverage is not standardized.

## Fixed-length board estimates

When every board has a known fixed length and width, calculate one board’s area:

**Board area = board length × board width**

A fixed board measuring 6 feet by 5 inches is 6 feet by 5 ÷ 12 foot:

**6 × (5 ÷ 12) = 2.5 sq ft per board**

For a separate 150-square-foot measured area before allowance:

**150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 boards**

This is a manual calculation a reader can perform when actual fixed board dimensions are known; the Hardwood Flooring Calculator does not calculate board counts. Apply the project allowance to the full-precision result and round the final whole-board estimate upward. Check the result against the product’s actual packaging information before relying on it.

## Random-length hardwood

Random-length products may be packaged in cartons, boxes, or bundles containing pieces of varying lengths. When the package states total area coverage, that coverage can be known even though the number and length mix of boards varies. In that situation, reporting one exact board count would imply precision the product does not provide. Use required area and the stated package coverage instead.

The installer’s layout determines how board lengths are selected, staggered, cut, and reused. An area calculation cannot predict that selection.

## Other factors outside the coverage calculation

Hardwood quantity can be affected by direction, border or feature patterns, transitions, stairs, damaged tongues or grooves, visual selection, and site conditions. Product grade descriptions and installer decisions may affect which pieces are considered usable.

Fasteners, adhesive, underlayment, vapor control, trim, transitions, subfloor work, and finishing materials are separate calculations. Follow manufacturer instructions for installation method, site conditions, acclimation, and moisture requirements.

## Common mistakes

- Estimating exact board counts for random-length products
- Using carton coverage from a different width, grade, or collection
- Leaving closets or connected areas out of the measurement
- Treating one allowance percentage as mandatory
- Rounding required area or each room too early
- Rounding fractional cartons down
- Assuming all cuts or visually rejected boards can be reused
- Including accessories in the flooring coverage figure
